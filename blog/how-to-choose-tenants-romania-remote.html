How to Choose the Right Tenant in Romania When You Live Abroad

October 2026 9 min read Târgu Mureș, Transylvania By Velox Living

If you own a flat in Târgu Mureș but live in Germany, Spain, the UK or anywhere else, one decision matters more than any other: who you hand the keys to. Rent levels, renovation choices and even management fees are all recoverable mistakes. A bad tenant often is not. Under the Romanian Civil Code, a tenant cannot be evicted without a court order, and that process can take several months — months of lost rent, legal costs and possible damage, all managed from another country.

The good news is that most bad tenancies are predictable. The warning signs are usually visible before the lease is signed, if you know where to look and take the time to look properly. This guide explains what a reliable tenant looks like in the Romanian market, which documents to request, the red flags to take seriously, how to run a viewing and interview from abroad, and the lease clauses that give you the most protection if something does go wrong.

What Makes a Good Tenant in the Romanian Rental Market

Târgu Mureș is a long-term rental market. Most tenants are students, medical professionals and people working locally, and many stay for one to several years. That is good news for owners — but only if the person you choose is genuinely able and willing to stay and pay. For the wider market picture, see our complete guide to property management and rental yields in Târgu Mureș.

Common Tenant Profiles in Târgu Mureș

  • Medical and pharmacy students. The university attracts students on multi-year programmes. They are often quiet, long-term tenants, but usually depend on parents for rent — which is why a guarantor matters.
  • Doctors, residents and healthcare staff. The city's hospitals and clinics employ a large professional workforce with regular, documentable income.
  • Local employees. People working in industry, services and public administration, typically on Romanian employment contracts and looking for a stable home.
  • Relocating professionals and families. People moving to the city for a new job, who often need a lease quickly and can usually provide strong employer documentation.

Stability Signals That Matter Most

Whatever the profile, the same signals predict a successful tenancy:

  • Verifiable, regular income that comfortably covers rent plus utilities and building association costs. An employment contract for an indefinite period (contract de muncă pe perioadă nedeterminată) is a stronger signal than a short fixed-term one.
  • A coherent story. The reason for moving, the timing and the intended length of stay should make sense together and match the documents.
  • A positive rental history that a previous landlord is willing to confirm.
  • Clear, consistent communication — answering questions directly and providing documents without repeated chasing.
  • Realistic expectations about the property, the rent and the house rules, so that disagreements do not start in month one.

Documents to Request Before Signing

Every serious applicant should be able to provide the following. A tenant who expects to pay rent reliably will not be offended by being asked — reasonable people understand that an owner needs to know who is living in their home.

  1. Identity document. A Romanian identity card (carte de identitate) or a valid passport for foreign nationals. The name and details must match exactly what will appear on the lease. A written lease requires full identification of both parties.
  2. Employment contract or employer certificate. A copy of the employment contract or a salary certificate issued by the employer (adeverință de salariu) confirming position, contract type and income.
  3. Last three payslips or bank statements showing salary being paid. This confirms that the income on paper is actually being received.
  4. References. Contact details for a previous landlord and, where possible, the employer. A reference is only useful if you actually call it.
  5. For students: proof of enrolment and the identity and income documents of the guarantor, usually a parent.
  6. For self-employed applicants: evidence of regular business income, such as recent bank statements or tax filings, in place of payslips.
Handle Tenant Data Carefully

Copies of ID cards and payslips are personal data protected under the EU's GDPR. Ask only for what you need to make a decision, store it securely, don't forward it around family group chats, and delete the files of applicants you don't select. A professional manager will already have a process for this.

Verify, Don't Just Collect

A folder of PDFs proves very little on its own. Check that names, addresses and employer details are consistent across every document. Call the employer on a number you have found independently, not one printed on the document itself. Speak to the previous landlord and ask direct questions: did the tenant pay on time, did they leave the property in good condition, would you rent to them again? An honest "yes" to the last question is worth more than any certificate.

Red Flags to Watch For

One red flag on its own is not always disqualifying — there is sometimes an innocent explanation. Two or more together should make you stop and look much more closely.

Behavioural Red Flags

  • Pressure to sign immediately, or reluctance to allow time for checks. Good tenants plan their moves; urgency is often a sign that something else has gone wrong.
  • Asking for no contract, or for the contract not to be registered with ANAF. Every rental contract in Romania must be registered with ANAF within 30 days of signing. An unregistered tenancy exposes you to fines and leaves you without a usable contract in court.
  • Offering many months of rent in cash upfront in exchange for skipping checks. It feels like security, but it is a classic way to avoid scrutiny and leaves no paper trail.
  • Vague or changing answers about who will live in the property, why they are moving, or how long they intend to stay.
  • Interest in subletting or short-term rentals, or questions about whether "friends" can stay for extended periods.
  • Negative comments about every previous landlord. One bad landlord is bad luck. A pattern tells you something about the tenant.
  • Wanting to move in or receive keys before the lease is signed and the deposit has been paid.

Documentary Red Flags

  • Names, dates or addresses that don't match across ID, contract and payslips.
  • Payslips that look edited, use inconsistent formatting, or show an income that does not match the job described.
  • An employer that cannot be found or reached independently.
  • A "previous landlord" who turns out to be a friend or relative, or who cannot answer basic questions about the property.
  • Refusal to provide any documents at all, or only partial copies with key details hidden.

How to Conduct a Remote Viewing and Interview

Choosing a tenant from abroad is entirely possible, but it requires structure. The viewing has to happen in person in Târgu Mureș; the decision can be made from anywhere. Here is a process that works:

1

Pre-screen in writing

Before anyone visits, ask applicants for basic information: who will live there, occupation and employer, intended move-in date and length of stay, pets, and smoking. This filters out mismatches without wasting anyone's time.

2

In-person viewing by a trusted local

Someone you trust — a relative, a reliable friend or a property manager — shows the flat. They should note how the applicant behaves: punctuality, interest in the property, the questions they ask, and whether the people who arrive match the people described.

3

Video interview with the owner

Hold a short video call with the shortlisted applicant. Ask open questions: why this neighbourhood, why now, what their previous rental was like, how they plan to pay. Ask them to show their ID on camera so you can match the face to the document.

4

Verify documents and call references

Collect the documents listed above digitally, check them for consistency, and personally contact the employer and previous landlord. Allow 3–7 days for this — rushing it is how most screening mistakes happen.

5

Sign electronically, hand over in person

The lease can be signed electronically — a digital signature holds full legal validity in Romania under Law No. 455/2001. The key handover should still happen in person, with a signed handover report (proces-verbal de predare-primire) including photos and meter readings.

The Lease Clauses That Protect You Most

Screening reduces the chance of a bad tenancy; the lease limits the damage if one happens anyway. A Romanian rental contract must be in writing to be enforceable, and it should be registered with ANAF within 30 days of signing. Beyond the basics, these clauses do the most work for a remote owner. For a broader overview of tenant rights that every lease must respect, read the tenant rights section of our remote owner guide.

A Guarantor

A guarantor is a second person — typically a parent — who signs the lease and commits to covering unpaid rent or damage if the tenant does not. It is optional, but strongly recommended for students and for any applicant without a stable local income. The guarantor should provide the same ID and income documents as the tenant, and their obligations should be spelled out clearly in the contract.

Security Deposit

Romanian law does not set a maximum deposit, but market practice is 1–2 months' rent. The lease should state the amount, where it is held, and the conditions for deductions. The deposit is only as strong as the evidence behind it: a detailed handover report with photos at move-in is what allows you to justify deductions at move-out.

Penalty Clauses for Late Payment

A penalty clause sets out in advance what the tenant owes if rent is paid late, and sets a clear due date. It does not need to be aggressive to be effective — its main purpose is to make the payment date non-negotiable from the start. Have the wording drafted or reviewed by someone familiar with the Romanian Civil Code, as excessive penalties can be challenged.

Occupancy and Use

Name every person who will live in the property and prohibit subletting or short-term rental without your written consent. This single clause prevents a surprising number of problems.

Notice and Termination

For open-ended contracts, the landlord must give at least 30 days' written notice to terminate. Specify the notice period explicitly for both parties, and state the conditions — such as repeated non-payment — under which the contract can be terminated early.

Lease Protection Checklist
  • ✓ Written lease, registered with ANAF within 30 days
  • ✓ Guarantor signed on the lease (students and non-local income)
  • ✓ Deposit of 1–2 months with clear deduction terms
  • ✓ Late-payment penalty clause and fixed due date
  • ✓ Named occupants and no-subletting clause
  • ✓ Signed handover report with photos and meter readings

Professional Screening vs Going It Alone

Many diaspora owners start by asking a relative to find a tenant. Sometimes it works. But family members are rarely trained to spot an edited payslip, they may feel awkward asking for documents, and they are not available every time something goes wrong. Here is how the two approaches typically compare:

Step Going it alone from abroad Professional screening
Viewings Depends on a relative's availability Handled locally, on a fixed schedule
Document checks Often collected, rarely verified Cross-checked for consistency
Employment & references Frequently skipped Employer and landlord contacted directly
Lease drafting Generic online template Drafted in line with the Romanian Civil Code
ANAF registration Easy to forget from abroad Registered within the 30-day window
Key handover Informal, little documentation Photo/video inventory, signed report
If the tenant leaves early Start again from zero Re-letting handled for you
